The Clearing House and DTCC Have Named Their Networks

Two of the institutions at the centre of American payments and securities settlement have now named the technology they intend to build on. Both target the first half of 2027. Neither is live, and one widely reported detail does not appear in the source document at all.

By Kansas Prairie Webworks · September 29, 2026 · 5 min read
Educational technology commentary. Not investment, legal, tax, or financial advice.

Read aloud by your browser. Nothing is downloaded and nothing is sent anywhere.

Sources & verification (2)

The short version

On September 24, 2026 The Clearing House announced it had selected Quant to power its On-Chain Money Initiative, a network for clearing and settling tokenized deposit transactions. The Clearing House operates RTP and CHIPS, and its own announcement names both as systems the new network will connect to. It is expected to become available to participating institutions in the first half of 2027.

Separately, the Stellar Development Foundation describes a plan to connect DTCC's tokenization service to the Stellar network, resting on a no-action letter DTC received from the SEC in December 2025. DTC-tokenized assets are expected on Stellar in the first half of 2027.

Read the two documents next to each other and the interesting thing is not that institutions are moving on-chain. It is that the selection phase is over. Specific vendors are named, and both timelines land in the same six months. Read them closely and a second thing appears: the coverage is wider than the documents. The most repeated detail about the DTCC plan — that XLM has been picked as a settlement token — does not appear on the Stellar Development Foundation's own page describing it.

What The Clearing House actually announced

The announcement is dated September 24, 2026 and the verb matters. The Clearing House selected Quant. This is a vendor selection for a project it had already announced, not a merger, not an acquisition, and not a settlement of anything.

The described scope is narrow and specific. Quant supplies "the network's interoperability, orchestration, and transaction-management layer that coordinates the clearing and settlement of tokenised deposit transactions, while providing connectivity to existing fiat payment systems." RTP and CHIPS are named in that same passage as the existing systems being connected to.

That last clause is the part worth holding onto. The Clearing House is not describing a replacement for the rails it already runs. It is describing something that has to talk to them. Whatever else tokenized deposits turn out to be, in this design they arrive through an institution that already sits in the middle of US interbank payments, and they connect to the two networks that institution already operates.

The availability language is equally plain: the network "is expected to become available to participating institutions in the first half of 2027." Expected, and available to participating institutions — not launched, and not to everyone.

What the DTCC and Stellar document says, and one thing it does not

The DTCC plan is older than the September news cycle that swept it up. It was announced on May 27, 2026, four months before The Clearing House named Quant. It has not been covered here before, but it is not new.

The load-bearing fact is regulatory. According to the Stellar Development Foundation's description, DTC received a no-action letter from the SEC in December 2025 that "authorizes DTC to implement and operate a service that tokenizes real-world, DTC-custodied assets." The plan's timeline matches The Clearing House's: "DTC-tokenized assets are expected to become available on the Stellar network in the first half of 2027."

Two details are narrower than the reporting around them.

First, the asset scope. Russell 1000 constituents, ETFs tracking major indices, and US Treasury bills, bonds and notes appear on the page as examples under consideration, subject to further evaluation. They are not a committed list, and several outlets have printed them as though they were.

Second, and more striking: XLM is not named anywhere on that page. Not as a settlement token, not in any other capacity. A large amount of coverage has reported that DTCC selected XLM as a settlement token. The document those articles point back to does not say it. The network is named. The asset is not.

That gap is worth sitting with, because it is the ordinary condition of this subject rather than an unusual lapse. A plan to use a public network is a different claim from a plan to use that network's token, and the two get merged somewhere between the source and the headline.

The dates are the story, and they are both in the future

Strip out the vendor names and one fact is left standing. Two institutions, announcing four months apart, both expect to be available in the first half of 2027. Neither is running today.

That is not a criticism of either project. Settlement infrastructure is supposed to be announced years before it carries value, and a 2027 date published in 2026 is a normal engineering schedule. But it does mean that almost everything written about this in the present tense is describing a plan.

It also means there is a checkable claim on the record, which is rarer than it sounds. In six months it will be possible to ask a specific question with a specific answer: is either network available to participating institutions, as both documents said it would be? Most technology coverage does not leave behind anything that can be graded. These two do.

The useful posture between now and then is neither excitement nor dismissal. It is to note who committed to what, in writing, with a date attached — and to keep the receipt.

The network is named. The asset is not. Somewhere between the source and the headline, those two became the same claim.

The selection phase is over and the build phase has a date on it. The Clearing House picked Quant on September 24. DTCC's tokenization service is being connected to Stellar. Both documents say the first half of 2027, and both mean expected rather than delivered.

Two corrections worth carrying. The Clearing House selected a vendor; it did not settle with one. And the most-repeated detail about the DTCC plan, that XLM was chosen as a settlement token, is absent from the source document — a public network being named is not the same as its token being named.

Everything above is checkable in six months against what both institutions committed to in writing. That is the whole reason to write it down now.

Why Kansas Prairie Webworks is paying attention

I read vendor announcements the way I read comment files and cloture motions, which is to say slowly and with the original open in another tab. The pattern is always the same: a document makes a narrow, dated, conditional claim, and the summary of it makes a broad, undated, confident one. The distance between those two is where most of the confusion about this subject lives.

There is nothing here for a business in Central Kansas to act on. Tokenized deposits clearing through RTP in 2027 will not change how anyone takes a card payment next Tuesday. The transferable part is the habit. When a vendor tells you a capability is available, the useful questions are which document says so, what date it carries, and whether "available" means available to you. That is the same question whether the subject is interbank settlement or the software running your bookings.

Questions people are asking

Did The Clearing House settle with Quant?

No. The Clearing House announced on September 24, 2026 that it had selected Quant to power its On-Chain Money Initiative. It is a vendor selection for a project The Clearing House had already announced, not a legal settlement or an acquisition.

Is any of this live right now?

No. The Clearing House says its network is expected to become available to participating institutions in the first half of 2027. The Stellar Development Foundation says DTC-tokenized assets are expected to become available on Stellar in the first half of 2027. Both are expectations with a future date, not services in operation as of September 29, 2026.

Did DTCC choose XLM as a settlement token?

The Stellar Development Foundation's own page describing the plan does not say so. XLM is not named on it, as a settlement token or otherwise. Much reporting has stated that it was selected; that claim is not supported by the document those reports rest on. Naming a public network is a narrower statement than naming its token.

Which assets would DTCC tokenize?

The Stellar Development Foundation lists Russell 1000 constituents, ETFs tracking major indices, and US Treasury bills, bonds and notes as examples under consideration, subject to further evaluation. That is explicitly a set of examples, not a committed scope.

What is the regulatory basis for the DTCC plan?

A no-action letter DTC received from the SEC in December 2025, which according to the Stellar Development Foundation authorizes DTC to implement and operate a service that tokenizes real-world, DTC-custodied assets. A no-action letter is a statement that staff would not recommend enforcement action on the described facts; it is not a rule and not an approval of anything beyond what it describes.

What about Swift and Chainlink?

Deliberately not covered here. Swift has published material on blockchain interoperability and on its own shared ledger work, but swift.com refuses automated requests, so none of it could be read at source for this piece. The most widely circulated technical detail comes from a vendor's own blog rather than from Swift, and a source supplied to us during research turned out to be dated August 2023 — genuine, but three years old and about different work. A 2023 experiments release cannot support a 2026 claim, so the subject is left for a piece that can cite it properly.
