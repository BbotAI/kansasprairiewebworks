What I Think Is Going On With Tokenization

I didn't pick these networks because I like them. I followed the institutions and the regulators.

By Kaleb, Kansas Prairie Webworks · October 17, 2026 · 23 min read · updated October 21, 2026
My own research and opinion, not investment advice
Educational technology commentary. Not investment, legal, tax, or financial advice.

Read aloud by your browser. Nothing is downloaded and nothing is sent anywhere.

Sources & verification (76)

Why These Networks

I want to be clear up front about how this list came together. I didn't pick these networks because I like them. I followed the institutions and the regulators. These are the networks that the biggest banks, market utilities and financial regulators have named in their own announcements, filings and rulings as current information.

  • Canton Network: chosen by DTCC for live tokenized Treasury trades. Goldman Sachs built GS DAP on it, BNY and Goldman run tokenized money market funds on it, and J.P. Morgan deployed JPM Coin there. Visa, DTCC, Nasdaq, Circle, Broadridge, Tradeweb and Franklin Templeton help run the network as Super Validators.2,3,4
  • Chainlink: chosen by Swift for connecting banks to blockchains, and by DTCC for its Collateral AppChain. UBS, Euroclear and J.P. Morgan's Kinexys have used it.5,6
  • Quant: chosen by The Clearing House, which is owned by the largest U.S. banks, for its On-Chain Money network connected to RTP and CHIPS. Seven major UK banks used it for live tokenized pound payments. Quant's founder started the ISO committee for blockchain standards, ISO TC 307.7,8
  • Ripple and the XRP Ledger: given a national trust bank charter approval by the OCC, holds more than 85 licenses worldwide including full approval across all 30 European Economic Area countries, and the XRP Ledger is where Standard Chartered just started custody of Ripple's dollar stablecoin.9,10,11,32

And on the regulatory side, the SEC and CFTC now formally treat XRP, LINK and HBAR as digital commodities, not securities.1

That's the starting point for everything below. The institutions made these choices. My job is figuring out what those choices mean for the tokens.

The short version

Yes, they're really tokenizing. I don't think it's talk anymore. DTCC ran real production trades in July. UK banks moved real customer money. Swift's blockchain ledger went live with 17 banks. The ECB went live in September. The Clearing House picked Quant to build the U.S. network for 2027. And Ripple has spent billions buying the pieces of a regulated financial company: custody, prime brokerage, treasury software, payments and a trust bank charter.

The part I want to be straight about is this, right now most of the bank flows tokenize the assets and the money. They don't require the banks to hold QNT, XRP, Canton Coin or LINK. A lot of this is still sandbox and pilot work, and nobody pays full production fees in a sandbox.

What I've found is that the "working gears" for token demand already exist on some of these networks. Canton now makes its biggest operators lock up 70% of the coins they've earned. Chainlink converts institutional fees into LINK and locks it in a reserve. Quant's Fusion uses QNT as its gas token. The XRP Ledger has live AMM pools and a members-only exchange for regulated institutions. Chainlink set the bar by showing how to turn institutional revenue into token demand. I think the others will follow, and the next year will show which ones do.

Where the Regulators Stand

On March 17, 2026, the SEC and CFTC put out a joint 68-page interpretation that sorts crypto assets into five categories. They named 16 digital commodities that aren't securities: APT, AVAX, BTC, BCH, ADA, LINK, DOGE, ETH, HBAR, LTC, DOT, SHIB, SOL, XLM, XTZ and XRP.1,12

What makes XRP different is that it has both. It went through five years of litigation with the SEC, got a court ruling that XRP itself isn't a security when sold on exchanges, the case closed in 2025, and now it's on the joint commodity list too. No other token on that list went through a full court fight and came out with that kind of record. In October the CFTC also proposed trading rules that list XRP as a commodity.13

Three things I'd keep in mind:

  • LINK and HBAR are on the same list, so XRP isn't the only one with clarity. It's the one with the most tested clarity.
  • QNT and Canton Coin aren't on the list. The SEC said the list isn't complete, so that doesn't make them securities. It just means they haven't been named yet.
  • The release binds both agencies, but it's an interpretation, not a law. A future commission could revise it. The CLARITY Act in Congress would lock it in, and it's still pending.14

Ripple: What They've Been Building

When you add up what they've bought and the licenses they've stacked, Ripple is building a regulated financial institution, not just a crypto company.

The acquisitions:

  • Metaco (May 2023, about $250 million): Swiss custody technology. This became Ripple Custody, which banks use to hold digital assets.
  • Standard Custody & Trust (2024): a New York chartered trust company. This gave Ripple a regulated U.S. custodian.
  • Hidden Road (announced April 2025, $1.25 billion, closed October 2025): a prime broker that clears about $3 trillion a year for 300 plus institutional clients. Now called Ripple Prime.
  • Rail (August 2025, about $200 million): stablecoin payments infrastructure.
  • GTreasury (October 2025, about $1 billion): corporate treasury software that handles about $12.5 trillion in annual payment volume for companies like American Airlines and Volvo.
  • Palisade (November 2025): wallet and custody technology for institutions.15,16,17

That's about $2.7 billion in disclosed prices, and reports put it close to $4 billion counting everything.18,19

The bank charter:

On December 12, 2025, the OCC gave conditional approval for Ripple National Trust Bank, in the same batch as Circle, BitGo, Paxos and Fidelity Digital Assets. A national trust bank can do custody and hold the reserves for Ripple's stablecoin under federal supervision. It can't take regular deposits or make loans, and it isn't FDIC insured. Ripple has until around mid-2027 to open. Ripple has also reportedly applied for a Federal Reserve master account, which would let it hold money directly at the Fed. I've only seen that reported secondhand, so I'd treat it as unconfirmed. Small banks have sued to block these charters, so it isn't a done deal.9,20,21,22

The licenses:

  • More than 85 licenses worldwide.32
  • 55 plus U.S. money transmitter licenses, including Kansas.
  • New York BitLicense and the New York trust charter.
  • UK FCA electronic money license (January 2026).
  • Full MiCA license across all 30 European Economic Area countries (July 2026).
  • Singapore MAS and Dubai DFSA licenses.10,23,24,25

Custody clients:

Ripple Custody is used by banks including BBVA, DBS, DZ Bank and Intesa Sanpaolo. HSBC, BNP Paribas Securities Services and Societe Generale's digital unit also show up on client lists.26,27

RLUSD, Ripple's dollar stablecoin:

  • About $2.4 to $2.5 billion in circulation.
  • Backed by about $2.6 billion in reserves, with BNY as the reserve custodian and Deloitte doing the attestations.
  • On October 12, 2026, Standard Chartered began custody of RLUSD on the XRP Ledger.11,28,29

Ripple Prime:

  • Revenue has tripled since Ripple bought it.
  • Raised a $200 million facility from Neuberger Berman and $275 million in BBB rated notes.
  • Won a multi-asset brokerage mandate from Brevan Howard in October.
  • Launched round-the-clock perpetual swaps on real-world assets with Virtu on October 15, using RLUSD as collateral.
  • Clients can post RLUSD, XRP or tokenized assets as collateral.30,31,32,33

The company itself:

Ripple raised $500 million at a $40 billion valuation in November 2025 from Fortress, Citadel Securities, Brevan Howard, Marshall Wace, Galaxy and Pantera. A share buyback in March 2026 valued it at $50 billion. Revenue was about $1.2 billion, and Ripple Payments has processed over $100 billion.34,35,36

What that means for XRP, honestly:

This is the strongest case I've seen for Ripple as a company. It now has custody, a prime broker, treasury software for big corporations, payment licenses almost everywhere, a stablecoin with a top U.S. custodian and a bank charter on the way. That's a full stack.

The open question is how much of that business has to touch XRP.

  • Most of the new products settle in RLUSD or regular dollars, not XRP. The Virtu product uses RLUSD as collateral.
  • Ripple Prime accepting XRP as collateral is a real use, and so is the XRP Ledger being the home for RLUSD.
  • Reports say funds estimate about 90% of Ripple's net asset value comes from its XRP holdings. So Ripple's spending is largely funded by XRP, and Ripple has every reason to want XRP to matter.37
  • Ripple still controls about 31.4 to 31.8 billion XRP in escrow plus several billion in its own wallets. It releases 1 billion a month and usually puts about 700 million back. In October the net release was 300 million.38,39

If GTreasury's corporate clients, Ripple Prime's hedge fund clients and Ripple's bank partners start routing payments and collateral through XRP on the XRP Ledger, that changes everything. Ripple now owns the businesses that could make that happen.

What I've Confirmed Is Real

In the U.S.:

DTCC ran production trades in tokenized Treasuries on July 15, 2026, including repo, collateral pledges and securities lending. They did it on Canton Network and on their own private network, Besu, and Chainlink says it powered the collateral workflows. DTC takes the Treasuries it already holds, makes a digital copy, and delivers it to each firm's registered wallet. Those wallets are screened against sanctions lists, and firms can send tokens directly to each other. The SEC approval covers Treasuries, Russell 1000 stocks and the major index ETFs for three years.40

DTCC is also building a Collateral AppChain on Chainlink for round-the-clock collateral management, using Chainlink's workflow software (CRE) and its data standard. DTCC's own release says it's expected to go live in the fourth quarter of 2026.65 One report out of Sibos put it in early 2027, which I haven't confirmed.

The Clearing House picked Quant on September 24 to run its On-Chain Money Initiative. That's tokenized bank deposits connected to RTP and CHIPS, opening to banks in the first half of 2027. About 25 large banks are behind it.7

Globally, through Swift:

Swift connects more than 11,000 banks, and it has worked with Chainlink for over seven years.

  • 2023: tested moving tokenized assets across blockchains using Chainlink's CCIP, connected through Swift's existing infrastructure.66
  • January 2026: BNP Paribas, Intesa Sanpaolo and Societe Generale's FORGE unit settled tokenized bonds in a Swift trial, paid in regular money and SG-FORGE's euro stablecoin. Swift's announcement doesn't name Chainlink.67
  • July 9, 2026: Swift's blockchain ledger, built with Consensys, went live with 17 banks piloting tokenized deposit payments around the clock.5
  • September 28, 2026: Chainlink announced it's connecting banks to the Swift ledger and launched CCIP 2.0 with compliance checks built in.68

Chainlink takes a bank's standard ISO 20022 payment message and turns it into instructions a blockchain can carry out. Final settlement still happens through each country's payment system, like Fedwire.

In the UK:

Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, NatWest, Santander, Nationwide and Monzo completed live customer transactions in tokenized pounds on Quant's platform, including mortgage closings and a marketplace purchase. They plan three digital bonds in early 2027.8

In Europe:

The ECB launched Pontes on September 21. It lets banks settle tokenized deals in central bank money through T2. Deutsche Bank, Santander, Societe Generale and others are connected.41

The size of it:

As of October 17, 2026, RWA.xyz shows about $39.3 billion in tokenized assets that can move between wallets, about 0.03% of the $114 trillion at DTC. Tokenized Treasuries are about $14.7 billion. Stablecoins are around $291.7 billion.55,40

The Working Gears, Part 1: Who Does What

Every system needs five layers:

  1. Standards: ISO 20022 for messages, and ISO TC 307 for how blockchains work and connect. Quant's founder started TC 307.
  2. Messaging and orchestration: Swift's ledger, Chainlink CCIP and CRE, Quant Overledger and QuantNet.
  3. Data: Chainlink price feeds and valuations.
  4. Ledgers: Canton, Besu, the XRP Ledger, Ethereum and the banks' own ledgers.
  5. Settlement money: tokenized deposits, stablecoins like RLUSD, central bank money and the currency-swap step for cross-border payments.

Chainlink and Quant compete in places. Swift and DTCC picked Chainlink. The Clearing House and the UK banks picked Quant. Ripple sits mostly in layers 4 and 5, plus custody and brokerage on top.

The Working Gears, Part 2: How Each Network Rewards Holding, Staking and Liquidity

This is the part I think matters most for the tokens. A token only gets lasting demand if the network makes someone hold it, lock it, stake it, burn it or keep it in a pool to do business. Here's what each one actually does today.

Canton Coin (CC): a real lockup is already live

  • Fees on the public network are priced in dollars but paid by burning CC. New CC is minted every 10 minutes as rewards.42
  • Rewards go to three groups: Super Validators who run the core network, validators who process transactions, and featured apps that drive activity. Since January 2026, featured apps can mint up to 62% of the rewards.42,43
  • Validators used to get paid just for being online. That ended April 30, 2026. Now they only earn in proportion to the fees their users burn. So rewards now follow real usage.42
  • The big one: under CIP-0105, approved in March 2026, Super Validators have to lock 70% of all the CC they've ever earned to keep their status and rewards. Under CIP-0116, approved in May, featured apps have to keep CC locked too.44
  • A business has already formed around this. Canton Strategic Holdings, a public company, lends its own CC to Super Validators and apps to meet their lock requirement, and gets paid interest or a share of rewards. That's a yield market built on a required lockup.45
  • Visa is a Super Validator with the top weight of 10. DTCC, Nasdaq, Circle, Chainlink, Broadridge, Tradeweb, Franklin Templeton and SBI are among 56 listed Super Validators.3,4
  • The catch: new CC issuance keeps stepping down but never stops. It settles at about 2.5 billion CC a year. So the question is still whether burning plus locking outruns new coins.46

To me this is the closest thing to what I've been describing. The biggest institutions running Canton have to hold and lock the token to keep their seat.

Chainlink (LINK): set the bar

  • Chainlink lets customers pay in stablecoins or regular dollars, then converts that money into LINK through a Uniswap pool and puts it in the Chainlink Reserve. Chainlink says it doesn't plan to withdraw from the Reserve for years.6
  • The Reserve crossed about 6 million LINK in September, adding about 374,000 that month.69 Protocol fees were about $30 million in the first half of 2026.
  • Staking: a 45 million LINK pool paying around 4.3%, with a 28-day wait to unstake. Today the rewards come from a pre-funded pool, not from fees yet.70
  • The catch: the Reserve holds LINK, it doesn't burn it, and up to about 70 million LINK can unlock each year. So new supply still outweighs Reserve buying.

Why I say Chainlink set the bar: it proved a company can take dollar revenue from banks that never want to touch a token, and still turn that revenue into buying the token on the open market. The banks never hold LINK. Chainlink does it for them behind the scenes. Any of the other networks could copy that model.

Quant (QNT): the pieces are built, the rules aren't public yet

  • Fusion went live on mainnet June 2, 2026, connecting 74 networks. QNT is Fusion's native gas token, the way ETH is Ethereum's.47
  • Staking is designed for approved, identity-checked node operators who stake QNT against the nodes they run. Quant's documents also list the amount of QNT staked as a factor in which nodes get routed transactions.47,48
  • No slashing is planned. Unreliable nodes just lose their rewards.
  • What's not live or not published: the minimum stake, reward rate, lock period and launch date. The next step is testnet staking in Quant Connect.47
  • On the license fees, community researchers describe it this way: banks pay in dollars, Quant's treasury converts that into QNT and locks it for the license term. The lock is a dollar amount, rebalanced at renewal. If that's accurate, it's company policy, not code, and the terms are confidential.49
  • With only about 14.88 million QNT that will ever exist, a real fee or staking requirement would hit hard.

Quant has the parts. What I want to see is the Chainlink move: a public, verifiable way that bank fees become QNT that gets bought and locked.

XRP and the XRP Ledger: liquidity tools, no staking

  • There's no staking on the XRP Ledger, and validators aren't paid. Banks and others run validators because they use the network.
  • Every transaction burns a tiny bit of XRP. Only about 14.4 million has been burned in total, so that isn't a supply driver.38
  • Built-in AMM liquidity pools have been live since 2024. Liquidity providers earn trading fees.
  • A members-only exchange for regulated institutions went live February 18, 2026, with 82% of validators approving. Early pairs include Mexican peso and euro stablecoins against RLUSD.50,51
  • A built-in lending protocol is waiting on a validator vote. It has 19 of the 35 votes and needs 29 held for two weeks. Firms like Evernorth plan to lend once it activates.52,53
  • About 1.19 billion XRP sits in U.S. spot ETFs, about 473 million in corporate treasuries and about 408 million in DeFi.38,39

XRP's demand path isn't fees or staking. It's liquidity. If Ripple's payment, treasury and prime brokerage clients need XRP as the bridge or as collateral, market makers have to hold real inventory in those pools. The members-only exchange and lending protocol are exactly the kind of gears that would make that possible for regulated money.

Ethereum (ETH): where most tokenized assets already live

  • Ethereum holds more tokenized assets than any other network. RWA.xyz showed about $16.7 billion in distributed asset value on Ethereum on October 21, 2026. Against the roughly $39.3 billion total from October 17, that's a little over 40%. Ethereum also carries about $146.8 billion in stablecoins, around half of all stablecoins.56
  • BlackRock launched its first tokenized fund, BUIDL, on Ethereum in March 2024. Securitize is the transfer agent and BNY is the custodian.57
  • DTCC's private network, Besu, is built on Ethereum technology.40
  • J.P. Morgan rolled out its JPMD deposit token to institutional clients on Base, a network built on top of Ethereum, in November 2025, as reported by The Block.58
  • Smart contracts: every fund token, app and pool on Ethereum pays its fees (gas) in ETH, and part of every fee, the base fee, is burned.59
  • Staking: Bitwise counted a record 40.2 million ETH staked in July 2026, about 33% of supply. It said most of the new stake came from institutions like staking ETFs and corporate treasuries.60
  • The SEC's Division of Corporation Finance said in May 2025 that protocol staking isn't a securities offering. That's a staff view, not a rule.61

ETH already has the gears the others are still building: gas that has to be paid in the token, a burn and staking. The catch is the same one I see everywhere else. Banks on private Ethereum-based networks like Besu don't pay in ETH. The ETH demand happens on the public network.

Solana (SOL): institutions are paying attention

  • Western Union launched its USDPT dollar stablecoin on Solana on May 4, 2026, issued by Anchorage Digital Bank.62
  • Visa started letting U.S. banks settle with it in USDC over Solana in December 2025. Cross River Bank and Lead Bank were first.63
  • The Solana Foundation counts about $3.7 billion in tokenized assets on Solana as of late July 2026, not counting stablecoins. That includes BlackRock's BUIDL, Franklin Templeton's BENJI, and a State Street and Galaxy fund launched in May 2026. That's the foundation's own count.64
  • Bitwise puts Solana's staking ratio at 68%.60
  • SOL is on the same SEC and CFTC commodity list.

I didn't find Solana in the DTCC, Swift or Clearing House choices I followed. Where it shows up is payments and stablecoins. That's real institutional attention and worth watching, but most of that flow settles in stablecoins, not SOL. It's the same question I have for XRP.

Hedera (HBAR): worth a note

  • HBAR is on the same SEC and CFTC commodity list.1
  • SaucerSwap's Auto Pools are a good example of how to make providing liquidity easier for regular people: you deposit one token and the vault manages the price range. It reduces permanent loss but doesn't eliminate it.

The Pattern I See

Right now the gears are lined up like this:

  • Required lockup: Canton (live, for operators).
  • Fees converted into the token: Chainlink (live, still small).
  • Gas token and node staking: Quant Fusion (gas live, staking coming).
  • Institutional liquidity pools and lending: XRP Ledger (pools and members-only exchange live, lending pending).

Chainlink showed that institutions don't have to hold a token for the token to benefit from their business. Canton showed that a network run by institutions can still require them to lock up the token. I think those two models are the template, and I expect more networks to move that way once they leave the sandbox.

How I See Each Asset Class Working

Stocks:

  • Nasdaq got SEC approval to trade tokenized Russell 1000 stocks on the same order book and ticker as the regular shares.71
  • On September 17 the SEC gave a five-year exemption for trading tokenized stocks on public blockchains, including through AMM pools, with limits for the biggest stocks of 75 per venue and 0.25% of daily volume.72
  • No native token required today.

Treasuries:

  • The biggest piece. BlackRock, Circle and Ondo each have tokenized Treasury funds over $2 billion, and DTCC is making digital copies of real Treasuries.
  • Canton matters here because public network fees burn CC and operators have to lock CC. Chainlink matters through DTCC's collateral system.

Bonds:

  • Tokenized bonds beyond Treasuries grew from $0.6 billion to about $9 billion. The UK banks, the ECB, Swift and Chainlink are all active.

Commodities:

  • Mostly gold, about $5.9 billion. Pricing usually comes from oracles like Chainlink.

Derivatives:

  • The CFTC now allows tokenized Treasuries and money market funds as margin.73 Ripple Prime is pushing the hardest here, with round-the-clock swaps and RLUSD and XRP accepted as collateral.

Cash:

  • The other half of every trade: tokenized deposits, stablecoins like RLUSD, or central bank money.

How the Money Could Move Across Borders

  1. Dollars go into The Clearing House network, built by Quant.
  2. The instruction travels through Swift's ledger, with Chainlink connecting the blockchains.
  3. The currency gets converted through Ripple (XRP or RLUSD), another stablecoin, or the normal banking route.
  4. Euros come out through Pontes and T2.

Every piece exists now. Ripple's MiCA license across Europe and its U.S. licenses mean it's legally set up on both ends of that trip. What's missing is an announced link between these systems that names the software and the conversion asset.

The Liquidity Layer

I've been providing liquidity for several years, so I know how powerful it is and how it can hurt you.

Institutions are moving toward pools:

  • Uniswap added permissioned pools for screened traders.
  • The SEC's September exemption allows AMM pools for tokenized stocks.
  • BlackRock's Treasury fund trades on-chain through UniswapX.74
  • The XRP Ledger's members-only exchange is built for regulated firms.

Most institutional pools today pair tokenized Treasuries, stocks and stablecoins, not these tokens. Chainlink is the exception, since fees flow through a pool into LINK.

What I'd be honest about:

  • Pools sell the token as the price rises and buy as it falls, so they calm the price more than push it up.
  • Yield is only real if it comes from trading fees. Yield paid in new tokens is dilution.
  • Permanent loss is real when one side moves hard.
  • The CFTC charges brokers 20% capital on volatile crypto versus 2% on stablecoins, which keeps institutions in stable pairs for now.75

On Coinbase: it's connecting U.S. and international derivatives traders into one regulated liquidity pool after folding in Deribit. That's an order book for futures and options, not a yield pool. It means deeper trading and bigger moves, not required token use.76

Where I Land on Each Token

Chainlink (LINK):

About $9.7 billion market value. The most proven link between institutional revenue and token buying. The test is whether DTCC's collateral system and the Swift connection grow the Reserve faster than new supply.

Canton Coin (CC):

The strongest built-in mechanics: fees burn CC, and the biggest operators must lock 70% of their earnings. The test is whether usage burns and locks more than the network keeps minting.

Quant (QNT):

About 14.88 million total supply and around $2.7 billion market value. Fusion gas and node staking are the real hooks. The test is The Clearing House fee schedule and whether its network runs on Fusion.

XRP:

About $1.33 to $1.50 this week, roughly $85 billion market value, about 63 billion circulating. The most regulatory clarity of the group, and the company behind it has spent close to $4 billion building a regulated financial firm. The test is whether Ripple routes its own clients' payments and collateral through XRP liquidity.38,54

My Thesis

Every idea has to be built and tested. Nobody pays full production fees in a sandbox. Pilots run on test money and negotiated terms. So, I don't read today's lack of token use as the final answer.

When these networks go into full production in 2026 and 2027, operators can start charging real fees. If those fees are paid in the token, or converted into it, and the tokens get burned, locked or staked, then token demand ties directly to network volume.

That's not just theory anymore. Chainlink is converting institutional fees into LINK. Canton is making its institutional operators lock CC. Quant has made QNT the gas for Fusion. Ripple has bought the clients and licenses that could make XRP the bridge. Chainlink set the bar, and I think many will follow.

On top of that, if a network needs the token in liquidity pools for payments and trades to flow, market makers have to hold real inventory. That takes tokens off the open market for as long as they sit there.

Where I Know I Could Be Wrong

  • Some of this is already out of the sandbox, and DTCC, the UK banks, Swift's ledger and Pontes still don't require a token.
  • Banks don't want price risk on a volatile coin, which is why fees get priced in dollars.
  • Chainlink's Reserve is still smaller than the new LINK supply.
  • Canton keeps minting new coins forever, even at the lower rate.
  • Quant's fee and staking rules are private or unpublished.
  • Ripple's newest products settle mostly in RLUSD, and Ripple still controls a large share of XRP supply.
  • The SEC and CFTC interpretation could be revised by a future commission until Congress passes a law.
  • Token holders get no dividends, no votes and no cut of company revenue. Owning XRP isn't owning Ripple.
  • ETFs track price. They don't prove usage.

So I see the tokens as a bet on fee, lockup and liquidity rules that are only partly written, not a sure thing.

How I'd Value Them

Supply and demand, not stock math.

  • Fee side: tokens burned, bought or locked by real fees in a year, minus tokens created, released or sold.
  • Lockup and staking side: tokens that must stay locked for operators to keep their role.
  • Liquidity side: tokens that must sit in pools or with market makers for the network to work, and whether yield comes from real fees.

If usage takes more off the market than gets added, there's a real case. If not, the price is running on attention.

What I'm Watching For

  1. DTCC's Collateral AppChain going live on Chainlink, and Reserve buying after it does
  2. Canton's burn and lock totals versus new minting
  3. The Clearing House fee schedule and whether it touches QNT
  4. Fusion staking details: minimum, rate, lock period and reward source
  5. Ripple National Trust Bank opening, and the Fed master account
  6. The XRP Ledger lending protocol vote reaching 29 validators
  7. Any Ripple product that requires XRP for settlement, not just accepts it
  8. GTreasury or Ripple Prime clients moving payments through XRP
  9. Swift's ledger moving from 17 pilot banks into production
  10. Any institutional pool pairing QNT, XRP, CC or LINK with a stablecoin
  11. The CLARITY Act

Things I've Had to Correct for Myself That I Saw on Social Media

  • The Clearing House didn't settle with Quant. It hired Quant.
  • The viral "nothing will run unless you have the tokens" clip lines up with a 2019 interview.
  • The "QNT" leveraged ETFs track Quantinuum stock, not the Quant token.
  • Tokenized deposits are still fiat dollars.
  • Quant isn't in the ECB's Pontes launch group.
  • Coinbase's "global liquidity pool" is a derivatives order book.
  • Swift using Chainlink doesn't mean Swift's banks are buying LINK.
  • Chainlink's Reserve holds LINK. It doesn't burn it.
  • XRP isn't the only token the SEC says isn't a security. LINK and HBAR are on the same list. XRP is the only one that also won that point in court.
  • Ripple's trust bank approval is conditional. It can't take deposits or make loans, and it isn't open yet.

My Position

The infrastructure is real, and the institutions and regulators picked these networks, not me.

  • LINK set the bar by turning institutional revenue into token buying.
  • Canton has the strongest built-in lockup and burn.
  • QNT is the most sensitive to adoption. If production fees and staking land in QNT, the supply is so small that the effect on demand would be larger than for the others.
  • XRP has the clearest regulatory standing and a parent company that now owns custody, brokerage, treasury and payment businesses. If Ripple routes that business through XRP, the liquidity case gets very strong.

The way I read it, the infrastructure can win while a token doesn't, so what I'm watching most closely is which networks follow Chainlink's lead.

The institutions made these choices. My job is figuring out what those choices mean for the tokens.

So I see the tokens as a bet on fee, lockup and liquidity rules that are only partly written, not a sure thing.

Why Kansas Prairie Webworks is paying attention

Kansas Prairie Webworks builds websites, social posting and apps for businesses here in Kansas. The rails that money and records move on are being rebuilt right now, and the businesses we build for will plug into them sooner or later. I would rather understand that before it arrives than after.

Questions people are asking

Are institutions really tokenizing, or is it still talk?

Yes, they're really tokenizing. I don't think it's talk anymore. DTCC ran real production trades in July. UK banks moved real customer money. Swift's blockchain ledger went live with 17 banks. The ECB went live in September.

Do banks have to hold QNT, XRP, Canton Coin or LINK?

Right now most of the bank flows tokenize the assets and the money. They don't require the banks to hold QNT, XRP, Canton Coin or LINK. A lot of this is still sandbox and pilot work, and nobody pays full production fees in a sandbox.

Is XRP the only token the SEC says isn't a security?

XRP isn't the only token the SEC says isn't a security. LINK and HBAR are on the same list. XRP is the only one that also won that point in court.

Does Swift using Chainlink mean banks are buying LINK?

Swift using Chainlink doesn't mean Swift's banks are buying LINK. The banks never hold LINK. Chainlink does it for them behind the scenes.

How would you value these tokens?

Supply and demand, not stock math. If usage takes more off the market than gets added, there's a real case. If not, the price is running on attention.
